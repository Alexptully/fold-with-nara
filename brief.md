# Fold with Nara: content brief

The master brief for the deck. It sets what the deck must cover, the lesson content, every fact and its source, and the placeholders. Design and tooling rules live in `CLAUDE.md`.

## The assignment

Duolingo x Iovine and Young Innovator's Forum, Fall 2026, **Prompt 2: New World Character, Lesson Concept**.

> Your team's mission is to develop a new world character and create a concise, 10 question Duolingo lesson for beginners. Your proposal can either: 1. Introduce learners to an important cultural aspect of a country. Set them in a distinct location, such as a café, museum, monument, or building OR 2. Introduce the fundamentals of an entirely new subject that isn't already on Duolingo.
>
> Use design resources to keep consistent with the brand identity for characters and environments, especially the simple illustration style guidelines. Consider how your lesson will correct wrong answers and how your character can best encourage a learner to start something new and stay engaged!

We take option 1 (a cultural aspect of Japan). The lesson lives inside the team's origami pop-up from the shared doc, so the deck also answers the three questions the team's script was built around: what we teach and why, how the installation teaches, and the visitor's journey.

The pitch window is four minutes and should be interactive: the audience folds paper during the pitch.

## The user experience, in the team's order

"Your visit, step by step" follows this list from the team doc, one path node per item. The pop-up is free for everyone, so the doc's "free or paid" step is gone.

1. Welcome screen with fun animations, starring Nara the sika deer from Nara Park.
2. Choose what to fold, at three levels. Easy: Sakura and Mt. Fuji. Medium: Crane and Koi fish. Hard: Samurai helmet and the special Duo fold.
3. The station prints paper designed for that model.
4. Place the paper in the frame, as the screen in front of you shows.
5. The projector outlines where to fold, right on the paper.
6. Each fold comes with a question on the screen, and you answer it to move on. The questions are about the culture behind the item you're folding.
7. Scan with your phone to get a badge on your Duolingo account.
8. Keep the origami, or hang it in the installation.

The deck has no real renders left: "You folded Mt. Fuji" hangs the finished model in the same drawing of the tent's inside, above its back corner.
- Duo appears in text only, as a locked "special" option. The deck never draws Duolingo's characters or logo, so the kiosk drawings show a green dot for the locked Duo fold.
- The mini booth is the desk-size physical version the team built. It gets its own slide right after the kiosk and tent, so the presenter can point to it in the room. Until a photo is added, the slide shows the kiosk drawn at desk size.
- The mini booth has an iPad and a small projector, and both follow the deck live (see "The mini booth's iPad and projector" in the README). The iPad shows the kiosk screen for each slide, and on "Try the lesson" it mirrors every tap: the picked tile, a miss with Nara's hint, and the fold a right answer unlocks. The projector draws that fold's crease and arrow on real paper at its real size. Nara stays off the iPad until "Meet Nara", so the reveal happens on the slide and the kiosk together.
- The Mt. Fuji paper follows the team's sketch of the sheet: a blue square with dashed creases on both diagonals and a zigzag snowline across two opposite corners (top right and bottom left). Fold 1 folds it in half on the diagonal with the snowy corners on the outside, so both meet at the peak. Then it's folded down the middle and opened so it stands.

## The concept in one sentence

Nara, a sika deer from Nara Park, walks a visitor through ten stops in the park while they fold an origami Mt. Fuji; answers unlock each fold, so the visitor leaves with a mountain they folded and ten pieces of Japanese language and culture.

## The lesson

The featured model is Mt. Fuji, an easy model. The sheet comes printed with its dashed guide lines, so the visitor makes three folds, matching the team's fold photo. Saying hello (question 1) unlocks fold 1, so everyone starts folding right away. Questions 2 to 5 then unlock fold 2 and questions 6 to 9 unlock fold 3. Question 10 comes once the mountain stands, then the completion screen.

"Try the lesson" plays the question that unlocks each fold, in order: question 1, where the whole room says konnichiwa (fold 1), question 5 on what origami means, with a wrong answer first to show the hint (fold 2), and question 9 (fold 3). Each question opens with the paper as the previous fold left it; a right answer animates the next fold (projected crease and arrow, the flap folding over, then the new shape), and a Replay fold button plays it again. Finishing goes straight to the completion slide.
